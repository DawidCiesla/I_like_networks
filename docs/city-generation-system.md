# Persistent City Generation System

## Purpose

The city is no longer generated by the renderer from the current number of stops.

It is now a persistent simulation layer.

The core rule is:

```text
build transport infrastructure
→ unlock development area
→ reveal a persistent master plan
→ construct local roads over time
→ reserve valid parcels
→ construct individual buildings
→ keep everything permanently in the save
```

A new station increases development opportunity. It does not instantly materialize a finished district.

## State model

The save now contains:

```text
state.city
├── version
├── seed
├── timeSeconds
├── nodes
├── roads
├── districts
├── blocks
├── parcels
├── buildings
├── projects
└── nextProjectId
```

This is simulation state, not renderer state.

The renderer reads it and never decides which buildings exist.

## Deterministic master plan

At city creation, `generateCityMasterPlan(seed)` generates the full latent plan.

The current default seed is:

```text
284731
```

The same seed produces the same:

- district anchors,
- street graph,
- road geometry,
- blocks,
- parcels,
- zoning,
- parcel development ordering,
- street frontage.

The plan is persisted.

Future development changes statuses, not geometry.

## Latent versus built city

A district can contain planned roads and parcels long before the player can see completed buildings.

Typical lifecycle:

```text
LOCKED DISTRICT
    ↓
station purchased
    ↓
ACTIVE DISTRICT
    ↓
local roads queued
    ↓
road construction
    ↓
road built
    ↓
parcel becomes developable
    ↓
building project queued
    ↓
construction site
    ↓
building complete
```

Already completed objects are never repositioned or regenerated when later stations are purchased.

## World scale

The Bus Era now treats approximately:

```text
1 world unit ≈ 1 metre
```

Transport geometry is deliberately much larger than the earlier prototype.

Current stop-to-stop road distances for the authored Bus Era are approximately:

```text
Line 1: 368 m → 675 m → 755 m → 835 m
Line 2: 707 m → 691 m → 803 m
```

This is intentional. A bus stop must not sit three houses away from the next stop.

The space between stops is expected to contain:

- several local streets,
- multiple blocks,
- dozens of parcels,
- parks or infrastructure reservations,
- future tram / road upgrades.

The camera therefore supports much wider zoom-out than before. The city is a navigable world, not a network diagram that must always fit at 1× zoom.

## Road graph

The default Bus Era master plan now contains roughly fifty road edges, including arterials and local streets.

Each edge stores:

- stable ID,
- district,
- road class,
- source,
- from/to graph node IDs,
- persistent polyline,
- unlock contract,
- construction state and progress.

Road classes currently include:

- `arterial`
- `local`
- `service`

Primary bus corridors are represented as arterial roads.

When the player purchases the station that requires a corridor, that arterial becomes built immediately so the bus service has a physical road.

Secondary district streets are built gradually.

### Connected-growth invariant

A city road is never allowed to appear as an isolated road in an empty field.

Every local road stores one or more `parentRoadIds`.

Construction obeys:

```text
built arterial
    ↓
first district spine
    ↓
connected side street
    ↓
outer street
    ↓
optional connecting street
```

A road project cannot enter the active construction state until every required parent road is already built.

The first point of each local road is a real graph node shared with at least one parent road. For loop-closing roads, both branches must exist before the connector may start.

The renderer follows the same rule: a future street is not even shown as a planned dashed alignment until its parent road exists.

Road construction progress always starts at the connected endpoint and advances away from the existing street network.

The Bus Depot access road remains locked until the depot itself exists.

## District activation

The master plan currently contains eight districts:

1. Old Town
2. Market Square
3. City Park
4. University
5. Central
6. Riverside
7. Museum
8. Harbor

A district becomes active when its corresponding transport stop exists.

Activation:

- does not create completed buildings,
- enables its blocks,
- queues its local-road projects,
- starts accumulating development age and pressure.

## Blocks and parcels

Blocks are persistent groups of parcels.

Parcels store:

- stable ID,
- district and block,
- world position,
- footprint,
- frontage size,
- setback,
- zoning,
- density class,
- deterministic development order,
- nearest/frontage road,
- lifecycle status,
- optional building ID.

The rescaled Bus Era currently resolves to roughly one hundred valid parcels for the default seed.

Parcels are generated along real local-street frontage rather than from a small fixed list around each stop. This gives each station several blocks of potential development instead of only a few building slots.

### Plan-time validation

Candidate parcels are rejected before the master plan is finalized if they conflict with:

- any primary arterial,
- any future secondary street,
- any transport stop,
- Bus Depot reserved space,
- another accepted parcel.

This is intentionally done against future infrastructure too.

A building cannot later discover that the future road was supposed to pass through it.

Every accepted parcel also stores `frontageRoadId`.

A parcel cannot start development until that exact frontage road is built. It is not enough for some unrelated road in the same district to exist.

This creates the basis for future:

- addresses,
- driveways,
- entrances,
- parking access,
- building-generated car trips.

## Protected infrastructure reservations

The master plan contains persistent reserved sites.

Reservations are generated before parcel validation and therefore take precedence over normal development.

The current Bus Era includes at least:

- Bus Depot reservation,
- City Park open-space reservation.

A reservation stores:

- stable ID,
- type,
- world-space center,
- width / height,
- safety padding,
- facility type,
- lifecycle status.

Any parcel intersecting the protected rectangle plus its padding is rejected from the master plan.

This means a facility can remain unbuilt for a long time without losing its land to houses or shops.

The same system is intended for future:

- tram depots,
- rail stations,
- maintenance yards,
- park-and-ride facilities,
- major interchanges,
- substations,
- civic buildings,
- airport / ferry infrastructure.

## Time-based construction

City development uses explicit projects.

A project stores:

- type,
- target,
- district,
- queued time,
- eligibility time,
- start time,
- duration,
- progress,
- completion time.

Only two projects can currently be active globally at the same time.

This prevents an unlocked district from appearing all at once.

### Current early timing

At 1× speed, the starting area behaves approximately like:

```text
0 s      Old Town plan becomes active
5–25 s   local streets are constructed
35–40 s  first building starts construction
~50 s    first building completes
60–90 s  additional individual buildings follow
```

The exact order is deterministic but development speed can later be rebalanced.

## Development pressure

A parcel is not developed only because enough time passed.

District development pressure currently includes:

- district age,
- number of stops on its line,
- recent actual passenger arrivals,
- fleet size,
- interchange bonus,
- central-district bonus.

Conceptually:

```text
development pressure =
    age
  + transport coverage
  + actual ridership
  + service intensity
  + location bonuses
```

This is the foundation for proper transit-oriented development.

A later iteration can extend the same score with:

- population,
- jobs,
- land value,
- road congestion,
- nearby amenities,
- noise,
- accessibility to multiple transport modes.

## Building selection

When a parcel becomes eligible, the city derives a building profile from:

- zoning,
- planned parcel density,
- district type,
- current development pressure.

Examples:

- low residential → house,
- stronger residential → townhouse / apartment,
- mixed → shop / mid-rise,
- central commercial under high pressure → tower,
- civic near University → campus building,
- industrial → workshop / warehouse.

The chosen profile is persisted on the building project.

It is not rerolled by the renderer.

## Construction rendering

The renderer displays the state of the real project.

Building construction visibly progresses through:

- surveyed/fenced parcel,
- foundation,
- structural frame,
- completed building.

Road construction similarly shows:

- planned alignment,
- partially completed road,
- construction head,
- finished street.

This means city growth can be watched in normal gameplay.

## Save migration

Game state is now version 9.

City-state schema is version 2.

Older Bus Era saves migrate into the new city model.

Migration preserves the transport/economy state and creates a deterministic city master plan consistent with the already unlocked stops.

After migration, future development proceeds normally.

A save/reload round trip preserves the exact city plan.

## Architecture

```text
transportLayout.js
        ↓
planGenerator.js
        ↓
cityModel.js
        ↓
simulation/model.js
        ↓
state.city
        ↓
cityRenderer.js
```

### planGenerator.js

Owns deterministic spatial planning.

### cityModel.js

Owns lifecycle, development pressure, projects and construction timing.

### cityRenderer.js

Only visualizes persistent city state.

It does not create lots or decide which building should exist.

## Current scope

This milestone implements the foundation corresponding to:

1. persistent city state,
2. persistent road graph,
3. latent master plan,
4. planned district roads,
5. blocks and parcels,
6. time-based development.

Still deferred:

- parcel redevelopment,
- actual residents/jobs,
- buildings generating passenger trips,
- dynamic land value,
- road-capacity simulation,
- real car traffic and congestion,
- bus lanes,
- tram rails embedded into road edges,
- automatic procedural expansion beyond the authored Bus Era districts,
- city-generation debug/heat-map UI.

Those should build on the persistent model rather than reintroduce renderer-driven procedural decoration.


## Scale-and-connectivity revision

This revision replaces the earlier compact city layout.

Key invariants are now enforced by tests:

1. authored bus-stop road distance must stay within a realistic urban range,
2. every local road must reference an existing parent road,
3. every local road must share a physical graph node with its parent network,
4. construction may never begin before parent roads are complete,
5. every developable parcel must have a real frontage road,
6. a building may only develop after that exact frontage road is built,
7. protected infrastructure reservations may never overlap normal parcels,
8. built roads and buildings remain stable after later network expansion.

These constraints should be preserved when procedural city expansion is introduced beyond the current authored Bus Era.
